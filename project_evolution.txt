3. STATISTICAL DESIGN DECISIONS:
   - Scenario 1: Customer Support Representative Training Program (30 reps sample)
     - Test Selection: Paired Samples T-Test (Before vs. After). This provides a direct, highly intuitive baseline comparing CSAT (Customer Satisfaction Score) and AHT (Average Handling Time) before and after the training.
     - ANOVA Concept Extension: The notebook will include a dedicated educational section explaining how a simple Paired T-Test can scale into a Repeated Measures ANOVA if we were to track retention across three or more intervals (e.g., immediate post-training, 3-months retention, 6-months retention) to see if the training effect decays over time. This balances simplicity for HR while demonstrating deeper analytical knowledge.
   - Scenario 2: Marketing Channel Performance Optimization (A/B/C Testing)
     - We spend equal budgets across three marketing platforms: Facebook, X (Twitter), and TikTok.
     - Part A (Categorical Conversion Rate - Purchased vs. Not Purchased):
       - Test Selection: Chi-Square Test of Independence.
       - Rationale: A/B testing conversion rates deals with binary categorical successes/failures, so ANOVA is inappropriate. We use Chi-Square to compare conversion proportions and explain this critical distinction for educational value.
     - Part B (Continuous Order Value - Purchase Amount of buyers):
       - Test Selection: One-Way ANOVA followed by Tukey's HSD Post-Hoc Test.
       - Rationale: Comparing average order values across three independent platforms represents comparing the means of continuous variables across multiple independent groups, which is the exact domain of ANOVA.

1. RESEARCH & NUANCE DISCUSSIONS:
   - Discussed the simplification of p-values for executive/HR audiences.
   - Identified that describing p-value as "the chance experimental findings were a fluke/wrong conclusion" is a common layperson shorthand but statistically inaccurate (the inverse fallacy / prosecutor's fallacy).
   - Formulated a "bulletproof" YouTube script explanation: defining p-value as "the probability of observing an effect this large by pure random chance, assuming the training/campaign had zero real impact."
   - This keeps the explanation highly accessible to HR managers while remaining 100% statistically defensible under peer review.
